2. Project Introduction
2.1 Project Information
●​ English name: AI Interview Practice Platform
●​ Vietnamese name: Nền tảng luyện phỏng vấn chuyên môn bằng AI
●​ Team name: Group 5 - IB1902 log
●​ Software type: Web
Invera is a web-based practice platform focused on technical interview preparation. The
system provides role- and level-specific technical questions (e.g., fundamentals, common
problems, case studies) and evaluates user answers with clear criteria, then suggests
improvements and reference solutions.
2.1.1 Scope of Project
The scope focuses on technical knowledge and problem-solving practice: explaining
domain concepts, diagnosing common issues, and proposing solutions that match
interviewer expectations for a specific role and seniority level.
2.1.2 Method of Project
●​ Users practice with question sets that match their target position and level
(Intern/Junior/Mid).
●​ Users also practice with selected higher-level technical questions to stretch depth
and expose missing knowledge.
●​ For each question, the system provides context and constraints, collects the user’s
answer, scores it using a technical rubric (correctness, completeness, reasoning,
trade-offs, clarity), and returns: key missing points, a suggested improved answer
outline, and a reference solution or checklist.

2.2 Reason for Choosing This Startup Idea
2.2.1 Customer Pain Points
●​ Candidates often lack a structured way to practice technical questions that match a
specific role and seniority level.
●​ It is hard to know what depth and detail interviewers expect (what to mention, what
trade-offs to discuss, what common pitfalls to avoid).
●​ Self-study materials are fragmented and do not provide consistent evaluation of the
user’s own answers.
●​ Peer practice feedback quality varies and is not always available on demand.

5

2.2.2 Customer Pain Points
●​ Role- and level-aligned technical practice paths reduce randomness compared to
browsing question lists.
●​ Criteria-based scoring and checklists make expectations explicit and repeatable
across sessions.
●​ Reference solutions and follow-up questions help users learn what a strong answer
must contain, instead of memorizing fixed answers.
●​ Progress tracking by topic helps users focus on technical gaps rather than repeating
what they already know.

1. Market Analysis
1.1 Internal Analysis
1.1.1 Current and recent market size and growth of the market
In recent years, the job search support and interview preparation market in Vietnam has
expanded alongside the adoption of artificial intelligence in education and recruitment
services. Industry reports estimate that the AI services market in Vietnam reached
approximately USD 76.5 million in 2024 and is projected to grow at a compound annual
growth rate (CAGR) of 34.29% between 2025 and 2033 [3].This growth reflects increasing
demand for scalable, technology-based solutions in education and workforce preparation
rather than traditional, resource-intensive training models.
Demand within this market is closely linked to skill gaps among young job seekers,
particularly in communication, self-presentation, and interview performance. Reports
indicate that approximately 83% of students do not fully meet employer expectations
regarding soft skills [4]. This gap increases reliance on supplementary training tools that
can simulate interview situations and provide structured feedback.In addition, Vietnam
currently records around 1.4 million young people classified as NEET (not in education,
employment, or training), representing a large potential customer segment with a strong
need for job-seeking and skill development support [1]. Furthermore, the high level of
technology adoption, with approximately 81% of internet users having interacted with
AI-based tools, provides a favorable foundation for the development of online interview
training solutions [4].
Based on the TAM–SAM–SOM framework, the total addressable market is estimated at
over 4 million individuals annually, including university students, recent graduates, and
unemployed youth. The serviceable available market narrows this scope to students and
newly graduated individuals, estimated at more than 2.6 million people. In the short term,
the serviceable obtainable market focuses on approximately 126,000 newly graduated
7

students in major urban centres such as Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City, where recruitment
competition is high and digital access is strong [2]. This segmentation reflects a realistic
entry strategy that prioritizes adoption feasibility over maximum scale. Overall, the market
is currently in a phase of stable growth, with substantial room for further expansion of
AI-driven interview preparation solutions.
1.1.2 Analysis of customer needs, attitudes, and trends in purchasing behaviour
The analysis of customer needs and behaviour is based on internal survey data collected
from students and young job seekers.
Based on internal survey data collected from students and young job seekers, customer
needs concentrate on three core areas: structured interview practice, realistic question
exposure, and actionable performance feedback. Many respondents reported uncertainty
about how to practise effectively and, more importantly, how recruiters evaluate interview
answers. This lack of clarity limits their ability to identify weaknesses and improve
systematically after each practice session.
Attitudinally, respondents express openness toward AI-assisted interview preparation tools,
provided that these tools deliver clear value. Price sensitivity remains high, and willingness
to adopt is closely tied to perceived usefulness rather than novelty.
In terms of purchasing and usage behaviour, users prioritise low-cost or free online
solutions that are easy to access and reusable. Approximately 30% of survey participants
indicated a willingness to use AI or chatbot-based tools for interview practice, suggesting a
viable early-adopter segment. However, limited affordability implies that pricing models
must be tightly aligned with student budgets and clearly demonstrate short-term benefits.

practices.
Technological:

2. Target Customers
2.1 Target Customer Portrait
2.1.1 Target Segment
The project targets third- and fourth-year university students and recent graduates in
Vietnam (within 12 months after graduation). They are preparing for, or have recently
joined, job interviews for Intern, Fresher, or Entry-level roles.
Context / Daily Life
At this stage, they are close to graduation or just finished school. Most do not have a stable
full-time job yet and are searching for their first role. Many are joining real interview
rounds for the first time, and they feel pressure to enter the workforce early and compete
with many peers.
Goals
They want to prepare for interviews in a clear and structured way. They want practice that
matches a specific role (for example: Software Engineer, Business Analyst, Marketing
Executive). They also want to answer questions clearly, briefly, and with structure, instead
of answering emotionally or going off-topic.

2.1.3 Core Problems and Needs of the Segment
A main issue is the lack of a systematic method to prepare. Many are not sure how
recruiters judge answers, and they find it hard to evaluate their own performance.
Common pain points include:
●​ Answers are long and not well structured.
●​ Limited access to realistic interview questions.
●​ Little or no specific feedback after practice or real interviews.
●​ Unclear mistakes and unclear next steps to improve.
Expected outcomes
They expect role- and level-matched practice, simple and clear feedback, and a way to see
strengths and weaknesses over time.
2.1.4 Behaviour and usage context
Most preparation is self-directed: practising alone, watching interview videos, and reading
shared experiences online. Some practise with friends, but not often
Opportunities to practise with mentors or recruiters are rare.

Participants should meet at least one condition below:

●​ Interested in Intern / Fresher / Entry-level roles
●​ Have a clear need to improve interview skills

2.2 Survey Analysis
Questionnaire Design Method
The survey questionnaire was designed based on a combination of the 5W1H framework
and four customer analysis dimensions: demographic, behavioural, psychological, and
geographical factors. This approach allowed the research team to collect comprehensive
data, covering both personal characteristics and real interview preparation behaviours.
Specifically, the 5W1H framework guided the question design as follows:
●​ Who: Identifying participants as students, final-year students, and recent graduates
●​ What: Exploring participants’ interview experiences, challenges, and performance
in interview practice
●​ When: Examining interview practice frequency and preparation timing
●​ Where: Identifying living and study/work locations to assess recruitment
competition levels
●​ Why: Understanding reasons behind ineffective interview preparation and
psychological concerns

●​ How: Identifying preferred feedback and support methods during interview
practice

●​ Demographic: Age, education level, major, and career stage
●​ Behavioural: Interview preparation habits, practice frequency, and tools used
●​ Psychological: Anxiety level, confidence, and expectations toward an interview
practice platform
●​ Geographical: Living area, especially major cities
This combined approach enabled the team not only to collect basic data but also to extract
meaningful insights into user needs and problems.

3.2 Pricing
3.2.1 Why pricing is important
Pricing decides if Invera can earn enough money to operate and grow. If the price is too
low, Invera may not have enough budget to pay for product costs (AI calls, storage,
servers) and marketing. This can slow down growth and make the business unstable.
YC explains that price affects customer acquisition strategy. If the sales effort or marketing
cost is high, but the price is low, the business can waste money. In that case, the company
must raise the price or reduce the cost of getting customers [15].
YC also explains that pricing can bring a big impact when it is tested and improved. A
simple way is to try different price points and track results like conversion rate, sales
volume, and revenue. Then the company can choose a better price based on data [15].
Pricing is especially important for Invera’s target users. Survey-based findings in the report
show that the target customer persona practices irregularly, mostly practices alone, and
rarely receives professional or structured feedback. As a result, self-evaluation is often
based on “feeling” instead of clear recruiter-like criteria.
For this reason, pricing should reduce risk for first-time users and clearly show the value of
rubric-based, structured feedback.
3.2.2 Pricing Strategies
Free plan
Invera offers a Free plan to let new users try the product before paying. This is important
39

users do not fully trust a new tool at the beginning. A free trial lowers the risk of trying.
In the product portfolio, the Free plan is designed as a trial experience with 1 session only,
basic rubric scoring, and a short summary report.​
To make the value clearer in the first try, Invera will add:
●​ One rubric sample (to show what “good” looks like)
●​ One short report (to show strengths and weaknesses quickly)
Tiered pricing
Invera uses tiered pricing because users do not practice in the same way. Some users
practice only a few times and want simple feedback. Other users practice many times and
want deeper tracking and support. Tiered plans let Invera match each user’s need and
budget, while keeping the product easy to understand.
●​ Basic is for users who want structured feedback at a low cost. It focuses on the core
interview practice experience: clear rubric-based scoring and a simple session
report. This plan fits users who practice alone and need objective criteria instead of
self-judgement.
●​ Pro is for users who practice more often and want to improve step by step. It adds
stronger progress tracking and deeper analysis, so users can see patterns over time,
compare sessions, and understand what to fix next. This plan fits users who
intensify preparation when interviews are close and need more frequent practice
with clearer improvement signals.
●​ Premium is for users who need the highest level of support, especially when
interviews are near or roles are competitive. It provides the most advanced
feedback and priority support (and may include hybrid coaching). This plan fits
users who want the closest experience to recruiter-like feedback before the real
interview.
Overall, this structure supports the target behavior: practice becomes intense near
interview dates, and users need clear benchmarks and structured feedback to improve
faster.
Usage cap
Invera uses paid AI services, so usage limits are needed to control operating cost and keep
pricing sustainable. This also keeps the system fair for all users, because heavy users
cannot consume unlimited resources.
Sessions per month .​
Each paid plan includes a fixed number of interview sessions every month. This fits real
40

usage patterns because many users practice more when interviews are near. A monthly
session quota keeps the plan simple and predictable.
Per-session limits (usage limit).​
Each session has two separate caps:
●​ AI model token budget (covers both input and output tokens)
●​ Speech-to-text (STT) allowance (measured by transcription time, e.g., minutes)
Standard session definition.​
One standard session is defined as:
●​ 30 minutes
●​ 10 questions
Basic plan enforcement​
For Basic, the plan includes a fixed AI token budget per session and a fixed STT
allowance per session. When either limit is reached:
●​ If AI tokens run out, the system stops generating AI feedback and outputs for the
remainder of the session.
●​ If STT allowance runs out, transcription stops for the remainder of the session
(users can continue by typing, if AI tokens remain).
These rules must be stated clearly in plan terms so users understand the limits before
starting a session.
Pro and Premium.​
Pro and Premium provide higher per-session AI token budgets and/or higher monthly
session quotas, and may include higher STT allowances to support heavier practice. Exact
numbers will be finalized after cost testing and user testing.
Value-based pricing + price testing
Invera uses value-based pricing: pricing should feel small relative to the value users
receive. The primary value is structured feedback (rubric-based scoring and clear reports),
designed to help users improve faster than self-judgment.
Invera will test pricing over time using a simple 10-5-20 method:
●​ Start with a price where perceived value is about 10× the price.
●​ Increase the price step by step (e.g., 5% each time).
●​ Stop increasing when conversion begins dropping enough to lose about 20% of
deals compared to the previous price.
●​ Use real data (paid conversion and revenue) to select the best price point.

3.6.2 Product Development Process
Invera follows a simple MVP loop: build → test → release → learn → improve. The
flowchart shows how we move from an idea to a working product [20]:
1.​ User/market research: we collect user problems and common interview needs.
2.​ Define role/level rubric + question set: we define what “good” means for each
role and level, and create matching questions.
3.​ Design user flow + UI prototype: we design the user steps and key screens before
coding.
4.​ Build MVP features: we build the interview room, scoring, and session summary
first.
5.​ QA testing + rubric consistency check: we test the product and check that the
rubric gives stable and fair results.
6.​ Decision: Ready to release? If “No”, we go back to improve the rubric, UI, or
features. If “Yes”, we release.
7.​ Release to pilot users: we let a small group use the product.
8.​ Measure: we track activation, errors, user feedback, score clarity, and where users
drop off.
9.​ Learn + prioritize improvements: we choose the next changes based on real
usage, then repeat the loop.

preparing for Intern / Fresher / Entry-level interview rounds.

●​ Internal alpha build
